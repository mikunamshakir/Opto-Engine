A comprehensive guide to optometric specialties, practice areas, and the global definition of the profession according to the World Council of Optometry

WCO Concept of Optometry

"Optometry is a healthcare profession that is autonomous, educated, and regulated (licensed/registered), and optometrists are the primary healthcare practitioners of the eye and visual system who provide comprehensive eye and vision care, which includes refraction and dispensing, detection/diagnosis and management of disease in the eye, and the rehabilitation of conditions of the visual system."

— World Council of Optometry [citation:1][citation:6]

WCO Competency Framework for Optometry

In 2024, the World Council of Optometry released its new Competency Framework, replacing the previous 4-level model with 5 comprehensive competency domains that define entry-level optometric practice worldwide. [citation:10]

Domain 1: Refractive Error

Assessment and management of refractive conditions including myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism, and presbyopia. Prescribing optical corrections and managing progressive conditions.

Domain 2: Visual Function Assessment

Evaluation of binocular vision, accommodation, ocular motility, color vision, contrast sensitivity, and visual field function.

Domain 3: Ocular Health & Disease

Detection, diagnosis, and management of eye diseases including glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, macular degeneration, cataracts, and ocular infections.

Domain 4: Public Health

Population-based eye care, disease prevention, health promotion, and addressing vision impairment as a public health priority aligned with WHO initiatives.

Domain 5: Professional Practice

Ethics, communication, interprofessional collaboration, evidence-based practice, and continuous professional development.

All Fields & Specialties of Optometry

Optometrists practice in diverse settings including private practice, hospitals, community health centers, academia, research, and industry. Below is a comprehensive list of all optometric fields and subspecialties.

Primary Care Optometry

Core Practice

Primary care optometry is the comprehensive management of a patient's visual function, ocular health, and related health care. The primary care optometrist assumes ongoing responsibility for the total visual, ocular, and related care of the patient. [citation:7]

Scope includes: Routine eye exams, refractive error management, diagnosis of common eye conditions, prescribing corrective lenses, co-management with other healthcare providers, and patient education.

Practice settings: Private practice, community health centers, retail optical settings, HMOs, and military facilities.

Ocular Disease / Medical Optometry

Advanced Practice

Medical optometry focuses on the diagnosis, treatment, and management of eye diseases and systemic conditions affecting the visual system. Optometrists prescribe medications, perform minor procedures, and manage chronic ocular conditions.

Conditions managed: Glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, age-related macular degeneration (AMD), uveitis, keratitis, dry eye syndrome, and hypertensive retinopathy.

Key fact: There are over 275 systemic conditions with potential ocular involvement. [citation:2]

Pediatric Optometry

Specialized care for infants and children's vision development. Includes amblyopia (lazy eye) treatment, strabismus management, myopia control, and developmental vision assessment.

  • Amblyopia therapy
  • Strabismus evaluation
  • Myopia management
  • Pediatric contact lens fitting
Low Vision Rehabilitation

Maximizing remaining vision in patients with visual impairment that cannot be fully corrected by standard means. Focus on functional independence and quality of life.

  • Prescribing optical/electronic magnifiers
  • Eccentric viewing training
  • Lighting and contrast enhancement
  • Assistive technology consultation
Vision Therapy & Rehabilitation

Neuro-optometric rehabilitation for binocular vision disorders, accommodative dysfunction, and visual processing issues. Also serves patients with traumatic brain injury and stroke.

  • Binocular vision therapy
  • Convergence insufficiency treatment
  • Brain injury vision rehabilitation
  • Sports vision training
Cornea & Contact Lenses

Specialized fitting of contact lenses for regular and irregular corneas, including specialty lenses for keratoconus, post-surgical corneas, and orthokeratology for myopia control.

  • Scleral lens fitting
  • Orthokeratology
  • Keratoconus management
  • Post-refractive surgery fitting
Geriatric Optometry

Specialized eye care for aging populations, addressing age-related vision changes and conditions common in older adults.

  • Age-related macular degeneration (AMD)
  • Cataract evaluation and co-management
  • Glaucoma management
  • Falls prevention through vision assessment
Sports & Performance Vision

Enhancing visual performance for athletes and professionals. Includes contrast sensitivity training, eye-hand coordination, and specialized eyewear for sports.

  • Dynamic visual acuity training
  • Sports-specific eyewear
  • Reaction time enhancement
  • Depth perception optimization
Vision Rehabilitation for Special Needs

Optometric care for individuals with developmental disabilities, neurological conditions, and complex medical needs requiring specialized assessment approaches.

Occupational Optometry

Workplace vision assessment and protection, including computer vision syndrome management, industrial vision screening, and safety eyewear consultation.

Academic Optometry / Education

Teaching and training future optometrists in academic institutions, curriculum development, clinical instruction, and educational research.

Research & Population Health Fields

Ophthalmic & Vision Science Research

Advancing knowledge in optical science, visual neuroscience, ocular pharmacology, and clinical trials for new treatments and technologies.

Research areas: Myopia control interventions, glaucoma diagnostics, retinal imaging technology, contact lens materials, and artificial intelligence in eye care.

Public Health Optometry

Population-level eye health initiatives, disease prevention programs, health policy development, and addressing global vision impairment as a public health priority aligned with WHO's World Report on Vision. [citation:10]

Key initiatives: School vision screening programs, community outreach, uncorrected refractive error elimination, and diabetic eye disease prevention campaigns.
