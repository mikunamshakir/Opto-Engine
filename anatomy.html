A comprehensive exploration of the structural marvels of the human eye — from cornea to retina.

The Architecture of Vision

The human eye is a masterpiece of biological engineering, containing over 2 million working parts and capable of distinguishing nearly 10 million different colors.

Understanding ocular anatomy is fundamental for diagnosing diseases, designing optical instruments, and advancing vision science. Our interactive resources break down each component's function and clinical significance.

Key Anatomical Structures

Cornea

The transparent front layer that provides 65-75% of the eye's focusing power. Critical for refraction and protection.

Clinical: Keratoconus, corneal abrasions

Iris & Pupil

Colored part controlling light entry. Pupil diameter ranges from 2-8 mm, regulating retinal illumination.

Clinical: Anisocoria, iritis

Lens

Crystalline structure that changes shape for accommodation (focusing on near/far objects).

Clinical: Cataracts, presbyopia

Retina

Light-sensitive layer containing ~120 million rods and 6 million cones. Converts light to neural signals.

Clinical: AMD, retinal detachment

Macula & Fovea

Central retina responsible for sharp, detailed central vision (20/20). Highest concentration of cones.

Clinical: Macular degeneration

Optic Nerve

Bundle of over 1 million nerve fibers transmitting visual info from retina to brain's visual cortex.

Clinical: Glaucoma, optic neuritis
