Eye Physiology Charts

Comprehensive visual guides to phototransduction, neural processing, color vision, accommodation, and visual pathway dynamics.

1. Phototransduction Cascade

Conversion of light into electrical signals in photoreceptors
Step-by-Step Process (Rod Photoreceptor)
  1. Dark Current: cGMP levels high → Na⁺/Ca²⁺ channels open → cell depolarized (-40mV)
  2. Light Absorption: 11-cis-retinal → all-trans-retinal (isomerization)
  3. Opsin Activation: Rhodopsin activates transducin (G-protein)
  4. PDE Activation: cGMP phosphodiesterase hydrolyzes cGMP → cGMP ↓
  5. Channel Closure: Na⁺/Ca²⁺ channels close → hyperpolarization (-70mV)
  6. Neurotransmitter Release: Glutamate release decreases → bipolar cell signaling
Light + Rhodopsin → Metarhodopsin II
Transducin (Gt) activation
PDE → cGMP ↓ → Hyperpolarization
Recovery: All-trans-retinal → 11-cis-retinal via retinal isomerase (RPE65) in retinal pigment epithelium. Time: ~30-40 minutes for full dark adaptation.

3. Central Visual Pathway

Retina → LGN → Primary Visual Cortex (V1)
→ →

Optic Nerve → Optic Chiasm (partial decussation) → Optic Tract → LGN → Optic Radiations → Visual Cortex (Area 17)

Visual Field Defects
  • Optic nerve lesion: Monocular blindness
  • Chiasmal lesion: Bitemporal hemianopia
  • Optic tract: Homonymous hemianopia
  • Meyer's loop: Superior quadrantanopia
Parvocellular (P) Pathway: Color, fine detail, high acuity → V4 (color processing)
Magnocellular (M) Pathway: Motion, depth, low contrast → MT/V5 (motion processing)

4. Accommodation & Near Triad

Dynamic focusing mechanism of the crystalline lens
Near Reflex Components
  • Accommodation: Ciliary muscle contraction → lens becomes more convex (+power)
  • Convergence: Medial recti contract → eyes adduct
  • Miosis: Pupillary sphincter contracts → pupil constricts (increases depth of field)
Accommodative Amplitude (Hofstetter's Formula):
Minimum Amplitude (D) = 18 - 0.33 × age
Example: Age 40 → 18 - 13.2 = 4.8 D
Presbyopia occurs when amplitude < 3-4 D
Age 20: ~10D
Age 45: ~4D
Age 70: ~1D

6. Pupillary Light Reflex (PLR)

Autonomic control of pupil size in response to light
Afferent Pathway

Light → Retina (photoreceptors, bipolar, RGC) → Optic nerve → Optic chiasm (partial decussation) → Pretectal nucleus (midbrain)

Efferent Pathway

Pretectal nucleus → Bilateral Edinger-Westphal nuclei → CN III (oculomotor) → Ciliary ganglion → Short ciliary nerves → Sphincter pupillae (constriction)

Clinical Reflex Testing
  • Direct reflex: Illuminated eye constricts
  • Consensual reflex: Non-illuminated eye also constricts
  • Swinging flashlight test: Detects relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD / Marcus Gunn pupil)
RAPD indicates optic nerve disease (e.g., optic neuritis, glaucoma) or severe retinal disease.

7. Visual Cycle (Retinoid Cycle)

Regeneration of 11-cis-retinal in the RPE
  1. 11-cis-retinal + opsin → Rhodopsin (in photoreceptor outer segment)
  2. Light → all-trans-retinal + opsin
  3. All-trans-retinol transported to RPE
  4. Isomerization via RPE65 → 11-cis-retinol
  5. Oxidation to 11-cis-retinal → returns to photoreceptor
Key Enzymes:
• RPE65 (isomerase)
• Lecithin retinol acyltransferase (LRAT)
• Retinal dehydrogenase
Deficiency: Vitamin A deficiency → night blindness (nyctalopia)

8. Dark Adaptation Curve

Sensitivity recovery after bright light exposure

Rod-cone break at ~5-10 minutes. Full dark adaptation takes ~30-40 minutes.

Clinical Application

Prolonged dark adaptation may indicate:
• Vitamin A deficiency
• Retinitis pigmentosa
• Congenital stationary night blindness
