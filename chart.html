1. Cross-Sectional Anatomy of the Eye

Reference Chart

Schematic cross-section of the human eye (Public Domain / Wikimedia)

Key External & Internal Landmarks
  • Sclera - White fibrous outer layer
  • Cornea - Transparent anterior window
  • Iris - Colored diaphragm controlling light
  • Lens - Crystalline focusing element
  • Ciliary Body - Produces aqueous humor, controls lens shape
  • Retina - Neurosensory layer containing photoreceptors
  • Choroid - Vascular layer supplying nutrients
  • Optic Disc - Exit point of optic nerve (blind spot)
  • Macula & Fovea - Central high-acuity vision zone

4. Retinal Cell Distribution & Function

Photoreceptor Counts

Rods: ~120 million — Scotopic vision (low light, peripheral)
Cones: ~6 million — Photopic vision (color, high acuity)
Ratio: ~20:1 (Rods:Cones)

Rods 95%
Cones 5%
Distribution Map

Fovea: Only cones (approx. 200,000 cones/mm²) — highest visual acuity.
Peripheral Retina: Dominated by rods, sensitive to motion and dim light.
Optic Disc: No photoreceptors — physiological blind spot.

Color vision: Three cone types (S, M, L) peak sensitivities at 420nm, 530nm, 560nm.

6. Ocular Blood Supply & Venous Drainage

Arterial Supply
  • Ophthalmic artery (branch of Internal Carotid)
  • Central retinal artery → supplies inner retina
  • Ciliary arteries (short & long) → choroid, ciliary body, iris
Venous Drainage
  • Central retinal vein
  • Vortex veins (4-5) → drain choroid
  • Cavernous sinus (final drainage pathway)
Clinical pearl: Branch retinal artery occlusion (BRAO) vs. Central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO).
